# Image Annotator 1.0.0

The first release. A private screenshot annotator that runs in your browser: paste or open an image, mark it
up, hide what matters, and copy or download the result. It is part of the TaroKit developer tools and also
works on its own at its own address.

## What you can do
- **Open an image** by pasting (Ctrl+V or Cmd+V), dropping a file, or choosing Open image. PNG, JPEG, WebP,
  GIF (first frame), BMP and AVIF (where your browser supports it) open. Photos turn the way your camera showed
  them. Files up to 50 MB, and up to 4096 x 4096 pixels; a larger image can be opened scaled down.
- **Look closely.** Zoom and pan, fit to view, or see the real pixels.
- **Mark it up** with rectangles, ellipses, lines, arrows, a pen, a highlighter, text and numbered steps. Pick a
  colour once and every shape, line, text and step uses it (the highlighter and redaction keep their own); size and
  fill are per tool. Hold Shift to keep shapes square and lines straight.
- **Hide sensitive parts.** Solid redaction replaces the pixels completely and is the safe choice. Blur and
  pixelate are marked "Not secure" because they can sometimes be reversed. The toolbar counts the redactions
  in the picture before you export.
- **Change your mind.** Select and move or resize anything, change its style, duplicate or delete it, and use
  undo and redo.
- **Crop and pad.** Crop without losing the original, add padding and a background colour.
- **Share the result.** Copy the picture to the clipboard or download it as a PNG. The file holds one flat image
  and no metadata. The download is named after your file with "-annotated" added.
- **Work from the keyboard.** Every tool has a key; press ? to see them all.
- **Don't lose work.** The browser asks before you leave with changes you haven't copied or downloaded.
- **Work offline.** After one visit the tool opens without a network connection.
- **English and Vietnamese**, following the portal's language setting, and light and dark themes.

## Privacy
Your images are opened and edited only in this browser. Nothing you add is uploaded, stored or tracked. To work
offline, the tool keeps a copy of its own files in this browser; it makes no other network requests. Exported
images contain no metadata from the original. Blur and pixelate can sometimes be reversed; use solid redaction
for secrets.

There are no accounts, cookies, analytics or third-party scripts. The page's own security policy blocks every
connection after it loads.

## Supported browsers
The latest two versions of Chromium-based browsers (Chrome, Edge, and others), Firefox, and Safari (WebKit), on
desktop, embedded in the portal or on its own. Drawing with one finger is supported on the latest iPad (Safari)
and Android tablets (Chrome). The layout works down to 320 pixels wide. Automated tests cover Chromium and WebKit;
Firefox and tablets are checked by hand before each release.

## Known gaps
- **Safari, embedded in the portal:** the "leave this page?" warning doesn't appear in an embedded Safari
  frame, so unsaved work can be lost without a warning (D-028). Opened on its own, Safari warns as usual.
- **Safari, blur or pixelate right after the first visit:** a first-visit problem found in testing was fixed
  (ADR-0025), and is being confirmed on real Safari. If a copy or download fails, nothing unredacted is exported;
  try again.
- **Safari's clipboard** adds its own colour-space and size tags to a copied image. They hold nothing from your
  original. Downloaded files carry none.
- **Offline copies are separate:** opening the tool on its own does not make it work offline inside the portal,
  and the other way round. Each keeps its own copy.
- **Your file name** appears in the name of the downloaded file (for example `login-annotated.png`).
- **Not in 1.0.0:** HEIC input, JPEG or WebP export, saving projects, and images beyond the size limit without
  scaling down.
- Pinch zoom on a Safari trackpad, Vietnamese typing on Safari, and screen-reader support are being checked by
  hand on this release.
